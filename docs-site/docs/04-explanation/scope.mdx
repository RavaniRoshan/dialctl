## What dialctl is

dialctl is an **engineering utility**. It evaluates six deterministic pre-dial
rules against locally hydrated data, records every decision in a tamper-evident
hash chain, and gives you a browser workbench for offline drift analysis.

It is a compliance *aid*: it makes the six rules mechanical, testable, and
auditable. It does not interpret the law for you.

## What dialctl is not

- **Not legal advice.** dialctl does not tell you whether a campaign is lawful.
  It tells you whether a call passes six specific checks.
- **Not an official TRAI/DLT certification.** It is not issued, endorsed, or
  certified by TRAI, any telecom operator, or any DLT platform.
- **Not a determination of consent.** It checks that a consent record exists and
  is unrevoked. Whether that consent is validly obtained is outside its scope.
- **Not a complete compliance program.** The six rules are a floor, not a
  ceiling. Confirm your full obligations with qualified counsel.

## Privacy guarantees

dialctl is designed so that raw subscriber data does not persist:

- **Phone numbers are hashed on the way in.** Every number is normalized to
  E.164 and replaced with `HMAC-SHA256(secret_key, e164)` — 64 hex characters —
  before it reaches SQLite, the JSONL ledger, or the browser.
- **The decision path makes no network calls.** `evaluate_pre_dial` reads local
  SQLite and returns. Nothing on the hot path sends data anywhere.
- **The browser workbench is local.** Parsed records live in IndexedDB
  (`trai_a2p_audit_v1`); preferences live in `localStorage`
  (`trai_a2p_ui_prefs_v1`). Use **Clear stored data** to wipe them.
- **The hash chain is tamper-evident.** Editing a stored decision or skipping a
  sequence number makes `verify_hash_chain` fail at the changed record.

:::warning
Your obligations do not change because the data is hashed. The digest is only as
strong as your `HMAC_SECRET_KEY`; treat that key as a secret, load it from the
environment, and never commit it. See `SECURITY.md` in the repository.
:::

## Regulatory context

The rules in dialctl are shaped by the TRAI TCCCPR obligations around
commercial voice calling — declaration of calling line identities, the
`140`/`160` series split, calling-hour limits, inquiry windows, and complaint
handling. `LAW.md` in the repository maps each rule to its regulatory
motivation.

That mapping is traceability, not a legal opinion. If a rule's real-world
application is ambiguous, ask counsel — not the engine.

## Reporting a problem

For a security issue, follow `SECURITY.md` in the repository. For a documentation
error, open an issue on the project's GitHub repository.
